Step 4 — India residency & tax relief
RNOR, ROR, DTAA and Section 89A explained
Tap any card for the full details
RNOR status (years 1–2)
Foreign income including 401(k) withdrawals not taxed in India. Best withdrawal window.
ROR status (after ~2 years)
Global income taxable in India. 401(k) taxed at slab rates up to 30%. Use DTAA + FTC.
India–US DTAA
Article 20: private pensions taxed only in country of residence. Prevents double tax.
Section 89A relief
India defers tax on foreign retirement accounts — mirrors US tax deferral benefit.
US estate tax risk
Non-US citizens: only $60K exemption. 40% tax above threshold on US assets at death.
Step 5 — Pre-departure checklist
Don't leave the US without doing these
  • Move 401(k) to Fidelity / Schwab International (supports Indian addresses)
  • Plan return date — aim for April to maximise RNOR window
  • Open NRE + NRO bank accounts in India before departing the US
  • Open RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) account to hold USD post-return
  • File Form W-8BEN with your broker after arriving in India
  • File US departure-year tax return (Form 1040-NR for following year)
  • Continue annual FBAR (FinCEN 114) filings for all US accounts
  • Consult a US CPA + Indian CA familiar with cross-border NRI matters
  • Discuss US estate tax exposure with a US estate attorney before leaving
  • Claim Section 89A relief in India ITR to defer annual tax on 401(k) growth
